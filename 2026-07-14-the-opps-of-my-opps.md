Hi {{firstName}},

I watch the World Cup for the art.

Not the result. Not the table. Not the narrative the pundits construct around national identity and historical grievance.

The art.

Jérémy Doku receiving the ball on the left flank and treating the defender in front of him like a suggestion. Kevin De Bruyne finding Romelu Lukaku in a channel that wasn't open yet, delivering a cross with the kind of timing that makes you wonder if he's seeing something the rest of us can't. That's not passing. That's composition.

That's why Belgium is my team. Not the badge. The players.

---

Belgium lost to Spain. Spain face France next. I'll be rooting for France — not because I have particular affection for France, but because Spain eliminated the art I was following, so I want Spain eliminated.

The opps of my opps.

---

Norway lost to England. Jude Bellingham scored a brace against a team led by his close friend Haaland. There's something almost unfair about that, and also something completely right. Kane was relentless in the way Kane always is — not flashy, just tenacious. Together they played the game the way it's supposed to be played.

When Norway went out, I didn't follow the grudge.

I followed England.

---

Same trigger — a team I love loses. Opposite directions.

It turns out I'm not loyal to Belgium. I'm loyal to what De Bruyne and Doku do. And when I find it somewhere else — when another team picks up that frequency and plays it clearly — I follow it there.

England face Argentina next. Messi. The greatest living argument for football as art.

I don't know who wins.

I know what I'm following.

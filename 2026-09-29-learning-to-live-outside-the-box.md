Hi {{firstName}},

Seven days ago, Ajax disappeared.

I don't know what happened. The cage went on the porch. The screens came out. The food was refreshed. I kept calling for him, kept making R2-D2 noises in the yard, kept listening for his song.

And I kept writing it down.

---

What followed was seven days of documenting grief in real time — not after the fact, not once I had something figured out, but while it was still happening and still unresolved.

The two-boxes framework emerged on Day 2: one box contains what I cannot know (is Ajax alive? is he safe? is he coming home?), and one box contains what I already know (the joke, the baths, the astromech conversations, the fly I caught out of habit and had no one to give to). Nothing eventually discovered in the closed box can change what's already in the memory box. 

Day 3: memories kept arriving on their own — a jumping spider, a ringtone, a flock of swallows on the wire. By the end of the day, still sad, but able to hold it easier. I still have all of it.

Day 4 was the day I kept going anyway — got dressed, took the meetings, put on the beard net, made pizzas. More responsibilities. More reasons. Coming home late, I found poop on the shoulders of a track jacket I'd only had two weeks. Still plenty of evidence of how much he was here.

Day 5 brought memories of Princess Henrietta Rootbeer Float, a kitten I lost years ago, and the realization that Ajax is still in high resolution — and I want to record the ordinary while I can still see it clearly.

Day 6: *back to normal with no resolution*. The box may never open. I can increasingly live as though he isn't coming back without ever deciding what happened to him.

Day 7 landed somewhere I didn't expect. An absurd video game thought that made me laugh. The AJAX protocol — seven days before the programmer in my head finally noticed. And this:

*I don't think I'm done being sad. But I am done letting sad stop me.*

There's a difference between sadness being present and sadness being in charge. Moving forward doesn't require leaving him behind. There's enough room. Ajax can come with me.

There's life outside the box.

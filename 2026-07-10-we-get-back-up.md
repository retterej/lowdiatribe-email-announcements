Hi {{firstName}},

The Many Hands Skate Fam has a motto.

Skate first.
Skate hard.
Get back up.

We said it enough that it stuck. It became one of those things the kids repeat without thinking. Part rule, part encouragement, part identity.

---

We were at the skatepark not long ago. My nine-year-old grandson went down — the kind of fall that makes everything stop for a second.

Before any of us could say anything, his older brother was already there. Eleven years old.

"You can do it," he said. "We get back up."

Simple. Automatic. Certain. Not a speech. Not a lesson. Just something he knew.

---

That's when it hit me. I'd forgotten.

Not the words. The feeling.

The feeling of knowing — without thinking — that falling is just part of it. That you don't stop to evaluate the fall. You just get back up.

The job search has a way of grinding that down. You start measuring everything. Counting attempts. Second-guessing every step. You start treating the fall like a verdict instead of part of the process.

I stopped writing for a while too. Stopped drawing. Stopped picking up the guitar and the ukulele. Stopped exercising. I stopped doing everything. Well, I did binge watch all of Parks and Recreation, but I stopped showing up for the things that mattered.

---

But kids don't do that.

They fall. They feel it. They get back up. Not because they're tough. Because that's the game.

Getting back up isn't the recovery. It's the continuation.

---

This is the quiet work right now.

Not figuring everything out. Not finding the perfect move.

Just — skate first, skate hard, get back up.

Every day.

Hi {{firstName}},

I have a favorite mug.

Red, with a big white heart on the side. Sixteen ounces, squared-off handle — easy to hold, substantial in the hand. A Valentine's Day gift from my wife, years ago. I drank coffee out of it for years, the way you do with a mug that fits right.

About two years ago I switched from coffee to tea. A gut decision — literally — after my gastrointestinal system made its preferences clear. The mug made the transition with me.

A few weeks ago I noticed the staining.

Not a crack you could see from across the room. Not a dramatic fracture. Just a faint discoloration along a line that ran up the inside of the mug — the kind of thing you only notice when you're looking directly at it, in the right light, after enough time has passed to make it visible.

The crack was already there. It had been for a while. The staining was just the evidence.

---

Ceramic and boiling water have a complicated relationship.

Every pour is a thermal event — cold mug, boiling water, the sudden expansion of material that wasn't ready for it. Then it cools. Then you do it again a few hours later. Thousands of small temperature swings, each one imperceptible, each one leaving something behind.

The mug didn't fail. It absorbed what I asked it to absorb, over and over, until the cumulative effect of all those ordinary pours became visible.

The crack was always going to be there. I just didn't know it yet.

---

I saw myself in the mug.

Some strain builds silently. Not from a single blow. Not from one catastrophic moment you can point to and say: *there, that's when it happened.* But from repetition. From the thermal cycling of hope — the expansion of optimism, the contraction of disappointment, the expansion again because that's what you do, you try again. You get back up.

A heart that breaks from being optimistic doesn't break all at once.

It accumulates.

I didn't notice my own crack for a long time. There was no single morning I could point to. Just the slow building of evidence — a heaviness that arrived without announcement, a narrowing I mistook for focus, a kind of tiredness that sleep didn't fix.

The crack was already there. I just hadn't held it up to the light.

---

The mug sits on a shelf in my room now. I switched to a stainless steel tumbler — R2-D2, because of course. Insulated, so the tea stays hotter longer. The adjustment wasn't a concession. It was just honest maintenance.

The crack isn't the failure. The crack is the record.

And seeing it — really seeing it, holding it up to the light instead of pouring around it — that's where grace lives. Not in being uncracked. In knowing where the crack is. Because a crack you've acknowledged is one you can tend.

Ignoring it is the only way it gets worse.

Hi {{firstName}},

Christmas 1987. My younger brother was struggling in school, and my parents — in an act of motivated generosity — bought him a Nintendo Entertainment System to incentivize better grades.

I was doing well in school. I did not get a Nintendo.

I liked Metroid. I really liked Castlevania. But I loved Zelda.

---

Forty years later, Zelda is on my body. It started with my wife — Toon Link and Toon Zelda, the one everything else grew from. She's not standing behind him or waiting in a tower. She's helping him lift the Master Sword. Not watching. Not waiting to be rescued. Helping him carry the thing he needs to do what he came to do.

Then came the hearts — thirteen of them, across my chest. Seven children, six grandchildren. Thirteen reasons to keep going, carried literally over mine.

Then the Triforce, on the back of my left hand. 

Not long after I had it done, a friend noticed it and cracked wise: "Hey, did you save Hyrule?"

I said: "Yes. Every year since 1987."

He laughed. I wasn't entirely joking.

Then my children on my sleeve — each one chosen for who they actually are. Sons as aspects of Link, and one as Skull Kid wearing the mask, because Skull Kid isn't Link. He's the kid who got lost. Who picked up something too powerful for him and couldn't put it down. Who caused chaos not from malice but from loneliness. Daughters as incarnations of Zelda — Tetra the pirate captain who never needed the title to be formidable, Twilight Princess who gives her life force without hesitation, and the youngest as Midna in chaos form, which is also the Phoenix Force, because I didn't choose one or the other. They're the same thing.

---

The Triforce wasn't something you could take by force. Ganon tried that. It shattered in his hands, and he was left holding only the piece that matched what he actually was: power, without wisdom or courage to temper it.

Link doesn't start the game powerful. He starts with nothing but a willingness to go. He earns strength. He earns wisdom. He already has courage — it's the one thing he brings to the door.

That's the version of courage I keep reaching for. Not fearlessness. Not certainty. Just the willingness to walk into the dark room anyway, with whatever you have, and figure it out as you go.

The game has been teaching me this for 40 years. I've had more reason than usual to lean on it lately.

---

The 40th anniversary is coming. The live action movie arrives the weekend before my birthday. I am shivering with anticipation.

Did I save Hyrule?

Yes. Every year since 1987.

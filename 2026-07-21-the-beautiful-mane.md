Hi {{firstName}},

The most magnificent thing about the Spain vs Argentina final was Cucurella's luxurious mane of hair.

I say that with complete sincerity. For long stretches of that match, it was the most interesting thing happening.

---

England played France for third place. Rice scored 134 seconds in. Four goals in the first half — England playing like what I'd seen against Norway, the how mattering as much as the result, the art present in every move.

Then the second half started and England played like France had played the first. Mbappé's brace. 4-3. England were still winning but it felt like losing. Saka stepped up for the penalty at 86:40 — hat-trick for Bukayo — and England held on.

Final: England 6, France 4. The highest scoring third-place match in World Cup history.

They played a beautiful game, but took turns.

---

That's the complicated thing about following quality over loyalty. Quality isn't a constant. It comes and goes, even in the teams that carry it best.

What do you follow when the quality disappears mid-match? Mid-tournament? Mid-anything?

You wait. You watch. You trust that the thing you recognized was real, even when it's not showing up right now.

That performance against Norway was real. England just couldn't hold it against Argentina. Couldn't hold it in the second half against France either — until they could.

The quality was always there. It just takes turns.

---

Then came the final. Spain and Argentina played to a 0-0 stalemate through ninety minutes. Martínez made eleven saves — the most in World Cup history. Argentina hadn't managed a single shot on goal.

Ferran Torres scored 39 seconds into the second half of extra time. The twelfth shot on target got through.

Spain 1, Argentina 0.

Spain's system won the World Cup. The best team maybe, not necessarily the most beautiful.

Except for Cucurella's hair.

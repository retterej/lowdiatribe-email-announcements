Twenty-five days after Ajax disappeared, I was still finding evidence of him in places I didn't know he'd been.

A nickel fell out of the cage cover when I folded it up. A track jacket on the back of a coat rack turned out to have been a favourite perch. An elaborate mouse den underneath the big cage, built almost entirely from his feathers — which explained why I'd found so few of them despite his constant preening.

Even after he was gone, I was still discovering where he'd been. Still cleaning up his mess.

The remembering started with Ajax and kept going — back through other memories, other losses, other patterns. I found myself reconstructing as much of my personal history as I could recall. Thinking about my thinking about my thinking. All because a little bird disappeared.

I'd rather have Ajax sitting on my shoulder, trying to steal something he shouldn't have, than any revelation about my own consciousness. But, if I was going to lose an eye, I might as well gain some knowledge.

The box being closed doesn't reach backward. It only closes forward.

I got the time we had. And it was good.

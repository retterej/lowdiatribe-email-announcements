Hi {{firstName}},

Ajax flew out the door yesterday.

A barrel-roll, diving through the closing door Indiana Jones-style, and he was gone. I went after him immediately — out the door, around the yard, whistling. No sign of him. I knew the drill then. Valya had done this, while she was with us. Put a cage on the porch. Pull the window screens. Set out food. Wait.

Over four months I had watched him grow from a helpless fledgling — eyes just opened, beak gaping, entirely dependent — into a strong and capable bird. Physically, he was ready for the outside world. What he didn't have was fear. Not of dogs. Not of cats. Not of anything, because nothing in his world had ever given him reason to be afraid. Animals outside the house don't know our rules. They don't know he's mine, nor would they respect my authority if they did.

There were two fears sitting side by side while I waited. The first was urgent and physical. The second was quieter and harder to hold: that he might simply not come back. Not taken — just gone. Choosing the world. If he ever chose to leave and never return, I hope he would be happy. I just wasn't ready for that to be yesterday.

---

He came back well before sundown.

Flew in through the open door and landed on my head as if nothing was amiss. No drama. No acknowledgment of the preceding hours. Just Ajax, back on his perch in the world, completely certain this was exactly right.

Ajax shrugged.

I had been holding up the sky. He had been having an adventure.

---

The grief that arrived with the loss didn't entirely leave when he returned. It just settled. Became something quieter. A reminder that I already know how this ends, eventually. He is a starling. I am not. The statistics are not in his favor for outlasting me, and I've known that since the beginning.

But yesterday was also a reminder of something simpler: he is here. Right now, today, he is here. That is not nothing. That is, in fact, everything.

I am revelling in his wonder. I know it while I am in it — that this is sacred, that this is rare, that not everyone gets this. I am paying attention. Every moment becomes a memory I already own. And when he isn't here, I will remember, and remember, and remember.

It's worth it. Every time.

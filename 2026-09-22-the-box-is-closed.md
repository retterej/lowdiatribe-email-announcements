Hi {{firstName}},

Yesterday morning, Ajax told a joke.

It went something like this: *"Whee wok shree chirpa chp chp. [pause] Chicca chicca buzz brrrr. [pause] Wikka wok woooooo."* Then he made a laughing noise. Then his alarm noise.

He landed the punchline and laughed at his own joke. I don't know what it meant in astromech. I know it was funny to him.

I made his breakfast. I turned the water on for his bath — he stood directly under the faucet, water pouring over him, completely certain that was exactly right, the way he always was. The joke, his breakfast, his bath. Just Monday.

Then he perched on the edge of the food bowls as I carried them over for the dogs' breakfast. Henri objected. I told him Ajax wouldn't eat his food, which was true and also beside the point — Ajax wasn't there for the food. He was there because that's where the action was. I set the bowls down. Ajax flew off to a shelf.

That was the last I saw of him.

---

We searched the house. Every room, every nook, every place he liked to perch and every place he had no business being. No signs of accident or struggle. No guilty looking cats burping feathers. He wasn't there. He wasn't anywhere.

The most plausible conclusion: he got outside. We know the drill — we've done this before. Cage on the porch. Screens pulled. Food out. Walk the yard, calling and whistling and making R2-D2 noises. Wait.

Hours passed. Nothing. The sun set. Nothing.

The sun rose. Nothing.

---

The mind won't let me grieve because hope is still technically on the table. And it won't let me hope cleanly because it knows the odds. So it runs both simultaneously — grief and hope, hope and grief — burning through a loop it cannot resolve. Schrödinger's bird. Alive and gone at the same time, until the box opens.

Except the box may never open. That's the part that's different this time.

Last time, I had a mantra to hold onto. *Until sundown.* It was enough, because he came back before it. This time sundown came and went. There is no threshold to wait for. There is just the open loop, running.

---

What I keep coming back to isn't the anger, or the grief, or the hope. It's the joke.

He told a joke yesterday morning — the same morning he disappeared. He had no idea that was going to be the last ordinary morning, the last dog bowl standoff with Henri, the last singing I'd hear from downstairs without knowing it was the last.

He didn't know. I didn't know. That's how it always works.

The joke is already real. The dog bowl standoff is already real. The baths — him standing directly under the faucet, water pouring over him — already real. The peanut butter sandwiches he'd sample without asking. The flies caught one at a time while he complained about the pace. The astromech conversations we were only just beginning to have. All of it already happened, already mine. The box being closed doesn't reach backward. It only closes forward.

The box is closed. But everything before it is still mine.

He was here. He was completely, entirely, unmistakably himself.

I keep listening for his song.

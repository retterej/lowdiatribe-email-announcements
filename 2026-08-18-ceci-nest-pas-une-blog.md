Hi {{firstName}},

My wife has been obsessed with Yungblud. I sat through the songs she played me — tolerant, pleasant-adjacent, probably reading as sullen. I had a ready verdict: derivative. *Oh, that's just Sabbath. That's AC/DC. That's Radiohead. That's Bowie.* I said it like it settled something. Our oldest granddaughter shook her head. I asked her if he was cringe, which she told me was itself cringe. I filed that away for future reference and kept my verdict.

Then one random Monday, a Lollapalooza after-show was announced. That Thursday. 11 pm in Chicago.

I bought tickets through a reseller and we went.

---

I want to be honest about what I brought to that night. I drove, because I'm the driver in our relationship. I endured the lines. I stood in the crowd while her enthusiasm moved at a frequency I wasn't quite tuned to. I was there, and I was good about being there, and I was probably not as good about it as I thought I was at the time.

The theater was one I'd frequented in my youth — Ministry on New Year's Eve 1989, Naked Raygun the following year, Front 242, KMFDM, Nine Inch Nails, They Might Be Giants, The Sundays, The The with the Cranberries opening. A catalog of a particular era.

Then Yungblud took the stage. He puts on an incredible show. I found myself paying attention.

---

The derivative thing is worth revisiting. Every artist I named as a source — Black Sabbath, AC/DC, Radiohead, David Bowie — was themselves synthesizing what came before. Human existence is derivative. We absorb our environment and make something new from it. That's not a flaw in the lineage. That's the lineage. I knew that. I just applied it selectively, to support my judgement.

---

Which brought me to a prejudice I'd been carrying closer to home.

I have bristled, for a year now, against calling *Low diatribe* a blog. The word felt dismissive — small, amateur, the internet's junk drawer. I preferred other framings. A resonance system. A transmission. Anything but the obvious thing.

But if it walks like a blog and talks like a blog.

Magritte painted a pipe and wrote beneath it: *Ceci n'est pas une pipe.* He was right. It isn't a pipe — it's paint on canvas. The name and the thing are not the same.

I've been refusing the name as if it could diminish the thing. But the distinction lives in the work, not in what I call it. Calling it a blog doesn't make it less. Those are my associations, not the thing itself.

The pretense was always mine to put down.

Even if I am rambling from the basement.
